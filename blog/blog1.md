## Why Data Science in Social Contexts Matters

Why Data Science in Social Contexts Matters

When I think about data science, the first things that come to mind are numbers, coding, graphs, and statistics. But I am starting to realize that data science is also connected to a lot of real-life problems that involve people and communities. Because of that, I think it is important to learn data science in a social and interdisciplinary way instead of only focusing on the technical side.

One reason we use data to study social topics is that it can help us notice patterns that are hard to see just by looking at individual situations. For example, data can help us study things like education, jobs, income, health, transportation, or access to resources. Looking at these patterns can help people better understand certain problems and possibly make better decisions. At the same time, I think it is important to remember that people are more complicated than just numbers in a dataset. A model can show trends, but it may not explain the full story behind why something is happening.

There are also a lot of responsibilities that come with using data about people. One of the biggest things I think about is privacy. Personal information should be protected and should not be used in a way that could hurt someone. Bias is another issue that data scientists have to be careful about. If the data is missing certain groups or is collected unfairly, the results can also end up being unfair. Because of this, I think data scientists need to pay attention to where their data comes from and how their work might affect different people.

Through this course, I hope to better understand how data science can be used to study real-world problems responsibly. I also want to learn how to look past just the numbers and think more about the people behind the data. I think this will help me become a better data science student and make better decisions when working with data in the future.
